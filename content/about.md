Biomimicry Hub documents how engineers, designers, and architects solve hard
problems by copying strategies that evolution has already tested — and what those
solutions became in the real world.

Every entry follows the same structure: the **biological mechanism** (what the
organism actually does), the **transferable design principle** (the part an
engineer can use), and the **human application** (what was built from it, named
wherever a real product or research programme exists).

## How the content is made

The site is built from a structured database of documented biological strategies.
Each organism page is generated from that database and then edited, so the factual
backbone — organism, mechanism, principle, application, real-world products — stays
consistent across the whole site.

Where a strategy originates from a catalogued entry, pages link to their source,
most often [AskNature.org](https://asknature.org), the biological strategy database
maintained by the Biomimicry Institute. Named products and research programmes are
attributed to the organisation that produced them.

The diagrams on organism pages are original work, drawn from the database rather
than reproduced from published figures.

## On accuracy

Biomimicry attracts a lot of loose storytelling — claims that a product was
"inspired by nature" when the connection is decorative rather than mechanical.
This site tries to stay on the right side of that line: the aim is that each page
describes a genuine transfer of a mechanism, with the engineering made explicit,
not a metaphor.

If you spot something wrong or overstated, it's worth telling us — corrections
are welcome.

## Affiliate disclosure

**Biomimicry Hub participates in affiliate programmes, including the Amazon
Services LLC Associates Program.** This means that when you follow certain links
on this site and make a purchase, we may earn a commission — at no additional cost
to you.

As an Amazon Associate, we earn from qualifying purchases.

Affiliate links are used for books and courses genuinely relevant to the topic of
the page they appear on. Recommendations are not paid placements, and a commission
never determines whether something is included. Pages are written first; the links
follow.
